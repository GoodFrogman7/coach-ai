# Serve Fundamentals

## Overview
The serve is the only stroke you fully control. It's the most important stroke in tennis.

## Key Phases

### 1. Starting Position
- Feet shoulder-width apart
- Front foot at 45° angle to baseline
- Weight balanced, relaxed posture
- Racket and ball start together

### 2. Ball Toss
- **Height**: 6-12 inches above full extension
- **Position**: Slightly in front and to right (for right-hander)
- **Consistency**: Same spot every time
- **Toss arm**: Straight, smooth lift (not throw)

### 3. Loading Phase
- Trophy position (both arms up)
- Weight shifts to back foot
- Knee bend for leg drive
- Racket drops behind back (scratch back position)

### 4. Acceleration
- Explosive leg drive upward
- Hip rotation forward
- Shoulder rotation (racket lag)
- Pronation begins
- Contact at highest point

### 5. Contact
- Arm fully extended
- Contact slightly in front
- Eyes on ball
- Pronation at contact for spin/power

### 6. Follow-Through
- Racket continues across body
- Land on front foot
- Deceleration controlled
- Recovery step into court

## Key Metrics

### Shoulder Rotation
- **Target**: 90-120° rotation
- **Why**: Generates racket head speed
- **Common issue**: Limited rotation = slow serve

### Knee Bend
- **Target**: 30-45° bend before leg drive
- **Why**: Leg power adds 30% of serve speed
- **Common issue**: Stiff legs = arm-only serve

### Contact Height
- **Target**: As high as comfortable full extension
- **Why**: Higher contact = steeper angle into service box
- **Common issue**: Low contact = higher net clearance needed = less pace

### Pronation
- **Target**: Forearm rotates through contact
- **Why**: Adds spin and protects elbow
- **Common issue**: No pronation = flat serve only, injury risk

## Serve Types

### Flat Serve
- Minimal spin, maximum pace
- Contact: Center-back of ball
- Use: First serve, ace attempts

### Slice Serve
- Sidespin, curves away
- Contact: Side of ball (3 o'clock for right-hander)
- Use: Wide serve, pull opponent off court

### Kick Serve
- Heavy topspin, kicks up
- Contact: Bottom-side of ball (7 o'clock)
- Use: Second serve, high bounce to backhand

## Common Errors

### 1. Inconsistent Toss
- **Symptom**: Ball in different spot each serve
- **Result**: Timing and contact inconsistent
- **Fix**: Toss practice (without hitting), mark target spot

### 2. No Leg Drive
- **Symptom**: Stiff legs, no upward explosion
- **Result**: Arm-only serve, less power, injury risk
- **Fix**: Emphasis on knee bend and leg drive, medicine ball throws

### 3. Elbow Leading
- **Symptom**: Elbow comes through before racket
- **Result**: Loss of racket lag and power
- **Fix**: Pronation drills, serve from knees

### 4. Poor Balance
- **Symptom**: Falling sideways or backward
- **Result**: Inconsistent contact, slow recovery
- **Fix**: Wider stance, better toss placement, core strengthening

## Biomechanical Sequence (Kinetic Chain)

1. **Legs**: Drive upward (30% of power)
2. **Hips**: Rotate forward (20% of power)
3. **Torso**: Rotation and extension (20% of power)
4. **Shoulder**: Rotation and internal rotation (15% of power)
5. **Arm**: Extension and pronation (15% of power)

Breaking the chain anywhere reduces power and increases injury risk.

## Training Priorities

### Beginners
- Consistent toss
- Contact at full extension
- Balanced finish

### Intermediate
- Leg drive integration
- Pronation development
- Second serve reliability

### Advanced
- Racket lag and speed
- Spin variation mastery
- Placement precision

## Serve vs Groundstrokes

Key differences:
- **Serve**: Overhead motion (shoulder stress)
- **Serve**: Vertical leg drive (not lateral)
- **Serve**: Pronation critical (not needed in groundstrokes)
- **Serve**: Timing completely self-controlled

Don't apply backhand/forehand expectations to serve biomechanics.

## Safety Note

The serve has highest injury risk due to overhead motion and high forces. Proper technique is essential:
- Gradual volume increases
- Adequate warm-up
- Pronation to protect elbow
- Leg drive to reduce arm strain
- Recovery time between high-volume sessions

If shoulder or elbow pain develops, reduce serve volume and consult a professional.
