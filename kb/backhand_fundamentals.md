# Two-Handed Backhand Fundamentals

## Overview
The two-handed backhand is one of the most reliable groundstrokes in modern tennis. It provides stability, power, and disguise.

## Key Phases

### 1. Preparation
- Unit turn: shoulders rotate together
- Early racket preparation
- Split step before opponent's contact
- Weight on back foot

### 2. Load Phase
- Hip and shoulder coil
- Racket drops below ball level
- Knees bent for power generation
- Core engagement

### 3. Contact
- Hip rotation drives the stroke
- Contact point slightly in front
- Both hands firm on racket
- Eyes on ball through contact

### 4. Follow-Through
- Full extension toward target
- Racket finishes high (over opposite shoulder)
- Weight transfers to front foot
- Recovery step back to ready position

## Common Issues

**Elbow Angle**: Should be ~150-160° at contact for optimal power transfer.

**Hip Rotation**: Minimum 40-45° rotation for power generation. Limited rotation = weak shot.

**Stance Width**: 2-2.5 feet for stability. Too narrow = balance issues.

## Pro Tips
- Practice shadow swings focusing on hip rotation
- Film from the side to check contact point
- Use targets to improve consistency before adding power
