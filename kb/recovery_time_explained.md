# Recovery Time: What It Means and Why It Matters

## Common Question Phrasing

This section covers: Why is my recovery time important? What is recovery time? How do I reduce recovery time? Why am I slow getting back to ready position? What causes slow recovery? How do I recover faster after shots?

## Definition

Recovery time is the duration from ball contact to ready position for the next shot. It's a critical indicator of movement efficiency and court coverage ability. Slow recovery means you're vulnerable to opponent attacks.

## Why It Matters

### Match Performance
- Fast recovery = better court positioning
- Good positioning = easier next shot
- Consistent recovery = sustained performance through long rallies

### Benchmark Times
- **Elite players**: 0.8-1.2 seconds
- **Advanced players**: 1.2-1.5 seconds
- **Intermediate**: 1.5-2.0 seconds
- **>2.0 seconds**: Vulnerable to opponent's attack

## Components of Recovery

### 1. Deceleration
After stroke, you must slow down momentum before changing direction.

**Technique**:
- Outside leg absorbs force
- Controlled deceleration (not abrupt)
- Maintain balance throughout

### 2. Direction Change
Transitioning from stroke direction to recovery direction.

**Technique**:
- Push off with outside leg
- Use small adjustment steps
- Keep center of mass low

### 3. Return Movement
Moving back to tactical position (usually center or anticipated next shot location).

**Technique**:
- Efficient path (not circular)
- Maintain ready position while moving
- Eyes on opponent

### 4. Split Step Preparation
Final component: ready to explode in any direction.

**Timing**:
- Execute split step as opponent contacts ball
- Feet shoulder-width apart
- Weight on balls of feet

## What Slows Recovery?

### Technique Issues
- **Over-hitting**: Excess power creates momentum that's hard to stop
- **Poor footwork**: Big steps instead of small adjustments
- **High center of mass**: Standing too upright
- **Watching your shot**: Not tracking opponent immediately

### Physical Factors
- **Fatigue**: Legs tire, recovery slows
- **Poor conditioning**: Lack of lateral speed/agility
- **Balance issues**: Unstable base requires extra time to stabilize

### Tactical Errors
- **Wrong positioning**: Moving to wrong spot wastes time
- **Late decision**: Hesitation adds delay
- **Passive mindset**: Not anticipating next shot

## Improving Recovery Time

### Technical Improvements
1. **Push-off technique**: Train explosive lateral push from outside leg
2. **Smaller steps**: Replace big strides with quick adjustment steps
3. **Stay low**: Maintain athletic stance throughout movement
4. **Immediate tracking**: Eyes on opponent instantly after your contact

### Physical Training
1. **Lateral agility drills**: Cone drills, ladder drills
2. **Plyometrics**: Box jumps, lateral bounds for explosive power
3. **Core strengthening**: Stability enables faster direction changes
4. **Endurance**: Conditioning to maintain speed when tired

### Practice Drills
- **Side-to-side drill with recovery**: Touch sideline, recover to center, repeat
- **Shadow strokes with recovery step**: Execute stroke, recover, split step
- **Reaction ball drill**: Random directions force quick recovery

## Fatigue's Impact

Recovery time is sensitive to fatigue:
- **Fresh**: Optimal recovery speed
- **Mild fatigue**: 10-15% slower
- **Moderate fatigue**: 20-30% slower
- **High fatigue**: >30% slower, technique breaks down

When Coach AI detects increasing recovery time:
1. Check for technique regression
2. Assess fatigue level
3. Consider training load adjustment
4. Prioritize recovery if high fatigue

## Session-to-Session Tracking

Coach AI measures recovery time from video:
- **Improving trend**: Physical conditioning or technique improving
- **Stable**: Maintaining efficiency
- **Declining trend**: Potential fatigue accumulation or technique regression

Use this data to:
- Validate training effectiveness
- Detect overtraining early
- Time competition entries (compete when recovery is optimal)

## Integration with Other Metrics

Recovery time interacts with:
- **Balance drift**: Poor balance requires longer recovery
- **Fatigue signals**: Often first metric to show fatigue
- **Match readiness**: Fast recovery = better readiness
- **Stroke quality**: Good technique enables faster recovery

Don't isolate metrics - view recovery time in context of your complete profile.
