# Forehand Fundamentals

## Overview
The forehand is typically a player's strongest weapon. Modern forehands emphasize rotation, lag, and racket head speed.

## Key Phases

### 1. Preparation
- Unit turn with shoulders
- Early racket take-back
- Non-dominant hand guides racket
- Split step before opponent's contact
- Weight shifts to back foot

### 2. Load Phase
- Hip and shoulder coil (separation)
- Racket drops below ball level ("lag")
- Knees bend for power
- Core winds up like a spring

### 3. Contact
- Hip rotation initiates (not arm swing)
- Contact point well in front of body
- Wrist firm at contact
- Eyes on ball
- Weight transfers forward

### 4. Follow-Through
- Racket wraps around body
- Full extension across body
- Deceleration over opposite shoulder
- Weight fully transferred to front foot
- Recovery step begins

## Key Metrics

### Hip Rotation
- **Target**: 60-80° rotation from load to follow-through
- **Why**: Hip rotation generates 60% of power
- **Common issue**: Arm-dominant swing with limited hip rotation

### Elbow Angle at Contact
- **Target**: 140-160° (semi-extended)
- **Why**: Allows power transfer + control
- **Common issue**: Too bent (weak) or too straight (loss of control)

### Wrist Angle
- **Target**: Stable through contact, then release
- **Why**: Firm wrist at contact = consistent strike
- **Common issue**: Early wrist break = loss of power and control

### Stance
- **Open stance**: Modern style, good for high balls and time pressure
- **Semi-open**: Balance of power and recovery
- **Closed stance**: Best for low balls and running forehands
- **Width**: 2.5-3 feet for stability

## Common Errors

### 1. Arm-Dominant Swing
- **Symptom**: Limited hip rotation, arm does all the work
- **Result**: Inconsistent power, prone to injury
- **Fix**: Wall drill focusing on hip turn, arm follows

### 2. Late Preparation
- **Symptom**: Rushed take-back, short backswing
- **Result**: Weak shot, poor timing
- **Fix**: Earlier unit turn, shadow swings with metronome

### 3. Inconsistent Contact Point
- **Symptom**: Sometimes early, sometimes late
- **Result**: Erratic ball flight
- **Fix**: Feed drills, focus on contact point only

### 4. Poor Balance
- **Symptom**: Falling forward/backward during stroke
- **Result**: Inconsistency, slow recovery
- **Fix**: Balance drills, wider stance, core strengthening

## Forehand vs Backhand

Key differences:
- **Forehand**: More hip rotation (60-80° vs 40-45°)
- **Forehand**: More arm extension at contact
- **Forehand**: Typically more power potential
- **Backhand**: Two hands provide more stability
- **Backhand**: Less rotation needed, easier consistency

Don't expect identical metrics between strokes.

## Grip Considerations

### Eastern Forehand
- Traditional, versatile
- Good for all ball heights
- Easier to transition to volleys

### Semi-Western
- Modern standard
- Excellent for high balls
- Natural topspin generation

### Western
- Extreme topspin
- Great for clay courts
- Struggles with low balls

Coach AI analyzes your biomechanics regardless of grip, but grip affects optimal ranges.

## Training Progression

1. **Fundamentals**: Consistent contact, basic rotation
2. **Power**: Increase hip rotation, racket head speed
3. **Spin**: Wrist lag and release, low-to-high path
4. **Variety**: Flatten out, heavy topspin, angles
5. **Match application**: Combine with footwork and tactics

Master each level before progressing.
