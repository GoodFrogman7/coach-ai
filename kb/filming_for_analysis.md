# How to Film for Best Analysis

## Camera Setup

### Position
- **Side view (most important)**: 90° perpendicular to baseline
- **Distance**: 15-20 feet away
- **Height**: Waist to chest height
- **Stability**: Use tripod or stable surface (not handheld)

### Frame Composition
- Full body visible throughout stroke
- Some space above head and below feet
- Enough width to capture full follow-through
- Consistent framing (don't zoom/pan during stroke)

## Lighting

### Best Conditions
- Outdoor: Overcast day (even lighting)
- Indoor: Bright, even court lighting
- Avoid: Backlighting (sun behind player)
- Avoid: Strong shadows that obscure body parts

### Problem Solving
- If one side of body is shadowed, move camera angle
- If background is too bright, adjust camera exposure
- Multiple light sources better than single harsh light

## Video Quality

### Settings
- **Resolution**: 1080p minimum (4K better for detail)
- **Frame rate**: 30fps minimum, 60fps ideal for slow motion
- **Focus**: Lock focus on player (disable autofocus if possible)
- **Codec**: H.264 recommended for compatibility

### File Management
- Short clips (5-15 seconds) easier to analyze than long videos
- Multiple strokes from same angle = consistent data
- Name files descriptively: date_stroke_view.mp4

## What to Capture

### Stroke Types
- Backhand: Side view is primary
- Forehand: Side view + optional front view
- Serve: Side and front views both valuable

### Number of Strokes
- Minimum: 3-5 strokes per session
- Ideal: 10-15 strokes for consistency analysis
- Include: Some easy rallies + some under pressure

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Too Close**: Can't see full body movement
2. **Handheld**: Shaky footage confuses pose detection
3. **Moving Camera**: Tracking player breaks analysis
4. **Cluttered Background**: Busy backgrounds reduce landmark accuracy
5. **Inconsistent Angles**: Can't compare sessions reliably

## Analysis Tips

### Before Filming
- Warm up thoroughly (cold technique isn't representative)
- Use consistent ball feed (toss, drop feed, or rally)
- Mark camera position for session-to-session consistency

### During Filming
- Focus on natural strokes (not exaggerated technique)
- Mix practice strokes with competitive intensity
- Film at different fatigue levels if studying endurance

### After Filming
- Review footage before leaving court
- Check: full body visible, stable frame, good lighting
- Re-film if quality issues detected

## MediaPipe Requirements

### What the AI Needs to See
- Clear view of all body landmarks
- Shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles
- No occlusion (don't turn away from camera mid-stroke)
- Contrasting clothing (solid colors better than patterns)

### Best Practices
- Wear fitted clothing (loose clothes obscure body position)
- Contrasting color to background
- Good lighting on all body parts
- Clean, uncluttered background

## Session Consistency

For best progress tracking:
- Use same camera position each session
- Same distance and height
- Same court location
- Same lighting conditions (time of day)

This allows session-to-session comparison and baseline analysis.
