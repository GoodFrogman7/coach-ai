# Training Load Guidance: Smart Session Planning

## What Is Training Load Guidance?

Training load recommendations help you choose the right training focus, intensity, and volume based on your current state.

## Session Types Explained

### Recovery Session
**When**: High fatigue, poor readiness, or post-competition
**Focus**: Active recovery, mobility, light movement
**Intensity**: Low (50-60% effort)
**Duration**: 30-45 minutes
**Example**: Light rallies, footwork at walking pace, stretching

### Technique Session
**When**: Moderate readiness, specific technical issues identified
**Focus**: Stroke refinement, drilling fundamentals
**Intensity**: Moderate (60-75% effort)
**Duration**: 60-90 minutes
**Example**: Shadow swings, fed balls, isolated stroke practice

### Movement Session
**When**: Technique solid but movement quality low
**Focus**: Footwork, agility, court coverage
**Intensity**: Moderate to High (70-85% effort)
**Duration**: 60-75 minutes
**Example**: Cone drills, defensive patterns, recovery training

### Conditioning Session
**When**: Good readiness, building physical base
**Focus**: Endurance, strength, explosiveness
**Intensity**: High (80-90% effort)
**Duration**: 45-60 minutes on-court + gym work
**Example**: High-rep drills, interval training, resistance work

### Full Training
**When**: Excellent readiness, no major concerns
**Focus**: All aspects, match simulation
**Intensity**: Variable (70-90% effort)
**Duration**: 90-120 minutes
**Example**: Technical work + point play + conditioning

### Match Simulation
**When**: Peak readiness, competition approaching
**Focus**: Competitive scenarios, pressure situations
**Intensity**: High to Maximum (85-100% effort)
**Duration**: 60-90 minutes
**Example**: Practice matches, tiebreakers, pressure points

## Intensity Levels Explained

### Low Intensity
- **Effort**: 50-60%
- **Heart Rate**: <70% max
- **Feel**: Conversational, sustainable indefinitely
- **Purpose**: Recovery, technique refinement

### Moderate Intensity
- **Effort**: 60-75%
- **Heart Rate**: 70-80% max
- **Feel**: Steady, can maintain for long periods
- **Purpose**: Skill development, aerobic base

### High Intensity
- **Effort**: 75-90%
- **Heart Rate**: 80-90% max
- **Feel**: Challenging, breathing hard
- **Purpose**: Match-pace training, anaerobic development

### Maximum Intensity
- **Effort**: 90-100%
- **Heart Rate**: >90% max
- **Feel**: All-out, unsustainable >60 seconds
- **Purpose**: Competition, explosive power development

## Decision Logic

Coach AI uses this decision tree:

### If Fatigue is High (>75)
→ **Recovery Session, Low Intensity**
- Rest is productive training
- Avoid technique work (fatigue prevents quality)
- Focus on mobility and restoration

### If Signal Trust is Low (<60%)
→ **Re-record recommended**
- Consider light Technique Session with new recording
- Low intensity (don't push on uncertain data)

### If Readiness is Excellent (>80)
→ **Full Training or Match Simulation**
- Opportunity for intense work
- High-quality practice produces best results
- Consider competition scheduling

### If Readiness is Good (65-79)
→ **Normal Training**
- Mix technique, movement, and play
- Moderate to high intensity based on fatigue
- Progressive overload

### If Readiness is Fair (50-64)
→ **Technique or Movement Focus**
- Address specific weaknesses
- Moderate intensity only
- Avoid high-volume or high-intensity work

### If Readiness is Poor (<50)
→ **Recovery or Light Technique**
- Reduce training load
- Low intensity only
- Investigate causes (overtraining? illness?)

## Focus and Avoid Areas

### Focus Areas
Lists specific metrics/skills to work on:
- Example: "Hip rotation", "Recovery time", "Balance"
- Drill recommendations target these areas
- Progress tracked session-to-session

### Avoid Areas
Lists what to rest or minimize:
- Example: "High-volume footwork" (if fatigue high)
- Example: "Intensive drilling" (if technique is fatigued)
- Prevents overloading weak areas

## Progressive Overload

Training should follow a cycle:
1. **Stimulus**: Challenging session (technique, movement, or conditioning)
2. **Fatigue**: Temporary performance dip, readiness decreases
3. **Recovery**: Lighter sessions, readiness rebounds
4. **Adaptation**: Performance improves above previous baseline

Coach AI helps you navigate this cycle:
- Hard days when readiness is high
- Easy days when fatigue appears
- Recovery before readiness drops too low

## Weekly Training Structure (Example)

### High Readiness Week
- Mon: Full Training (High intensity)
- Tue: Technique Session (Moderate)
- Wed: Match Simulation (High)
- Thu: Movement Session (Moderate)
- Fri: Recovery (Low)
- Sat: Competition or Match Sim
- Sun: Off

### Moderate Readiness Week
- Mon: Technique Session (Moderate)
- Tue: Movement Session (Moderate)
- Wed: Recovery (Low)
- Thu: Technique Session (Moderate)
- Fri: Light Match Play (Moderate)
- Sat: Off or Recovery
- Sun: Off

### Low Readiness Week (Recovery Week)
- Mon: Recovery (Low)
- Tue: Off
- Wed: Light Technique (Low)
- Thu: Recovery (Low)
- Fri: Off
- Sat: Light Match Play (Low-Moderate)
- Sun: Off

## Confidence Score

Training load recommendations include confidence:
- **High (>80%)**: All inputs reliable, follow recommendation
- **Medium (60-80%)**: Some uncertainty, use judgment
- **Low (<60%)**: Limited data, rely more on feel

Always combine data with self-awareness.

## When to Override Recommendations

### Trust Your Body
- If you feel great but readiness is fair → can train harder
- If you feel terrible but readiness is good → rest

### Life Context
- High stress, poor sleep → reduce intensity regardless of readiness
- Important competition soon → adjust training regardless of readiness

### Injury or Illness
- Always trumps readiness scores
- Rest and recover first

## Integration with Progress Tracking

Training load adapts to your trends:
- **Improving trend**: Gradually increase load
- **Stable trend**: Maintain current approach or add stimulus
- **Declining trend**: Reduce load, emphasize recovery

This prevents overtraining and optimizes adaptation.

## Key Principles

1. **Hard days hard, easy days easy**: Avoid "moderate everything"
2. **Recovery is training**: Adaptation happens during rest
3. **Quality over quantity**: Better to do less with excellence
4. **Listen to your body**: Data informs, you decide
5. **Progressive overload**: Gradually increase demands over time

## Realistic Expectations

- Not every session needs to be perfect
- Fluctuation is normal and healthy
- Recovery weeks are essential
- Long-term trends matter more than daily scores

Use training load guidance as a tool to make informed decisions, not as rigid rules.
