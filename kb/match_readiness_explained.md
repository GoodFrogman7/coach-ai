# Match Readiness: Understanding Your Competitive State

## What Is Match Readiness?

Match readiness is a synthesis score (0-100) that combines:
- **Technique quality**: Your stroke biomechanics
- **Movement quality**: Footwork, balance, recovery
- **Fatigue level**: Physical freshness
- **Signal trust**: Measurement confidence

It answers: "How prepared am I to compete today?"

## Readiness Levels

### Excellent (80-100)
- **Technical**: All strokes executing well
- **Movement**: Quick, balanced, explosive
- **Fatigue**: Fresh, no degradation signs
- **Recommendation**: Full training or competition ready

### Good (65-79)
- **Technical**: Solid with minor issues
- **Movement**: Functional with room for improvement
- **Fatigue**: Mild signs, manageable
- **Recommendation**: Normal training, competition viable

### Fair (50-64)
- **Technical**: Noticeable issues present
- **Movement**: Some limitations
- **Fatigue**: Moderate, affecting performance
- **Recommendation**: Technique focus, light competition

### Poor (<50)
- **Technical**: Multiple significant issues
- **Movement**: Compromised
- **Fatigue**: High, performance impacted
- **Recommendation**: Recovery or light technique work only

## What Readiness Is NOT

### It's Not Performance Prediction
- Doesn't predict match outcome
- Doesn't account for opponent skill
- Doesn't consider mental state or tactics

### It's Not Medical Diagnosis
- Not injury assessment
- Not health screening
- Not clearance to play

### It's Not Permanent
- Changes session to session
- Affected by training, recovery, technique work
- Can improve or decline based on your actions

## Using Readiness for Decisions

### Competition Timing
- **Excellent/Good**: Compete with confidence
- **Fair**: Competition possible but expect challenges
- **Poor**: Consider postponing if possible

### Training Planning
- **Excellent**: Opportunity for intense training
- **Good**: Normal progression
- **Fair**: Focus on weaknesses, moderate intensity
- **Poor**: Recovery priority

### Confidence Building
- Track readiness trends
- Compete when readiness is rising
- Build confidence through preparation

## Contributors Explained

### Technique (35% weight)
Based on stroke biomechanics:
- Hip rotation, elbow angle, wrist position
- Phase-by-phase execution
- Consistency across strokes

**Interpretation**:
- High technique score → Strokes are sound
- Low technique score → Technical work needed

### Movement (30% weight)
Based on footwork metrics:
- Split step timing and quality
- Recovery time
- Balance and stability

**Interpretation**:
- High movement score → Court coverage excellent
- Low movement score → Footwork/conditioning needed

### Fatigue (20% weight)
Based on degradation signals:
- Recovery time increases
- Balance instability
- Metric variability

**Interpretation**:
- Low fatigue → Fresh, sustainable performance
- High fatigue → Need recovery or load reduction

### Signal Trust (15% weight)
Based on measurement quality:
- Landmark visibility
- Tracking stability
- Data completeness

**Interpretation**:
- High trust → Reliable measurements
- Low trust → Re-record with better setup

## Confidence Score

Every readiness calculation includes a confidence score:
- **High confidence (>80%)**: All components present, reliable
- **Medium confidence (60-80%)**: Some components missing or uncertain
- **Low confidence (<60%)**: Limited data, take with caution

Always consider confidence when interpreting readiness.

## Session-to-Session Patterns

### Improving Trend
- Readiness scores increasing
- **Action**: Continue current training approach
- **Opportunity**: Schedule competitions

### Stable Trend
- Readiness scores consistent
- **Action**: Maintain current load, look for breakthrough opportunities
- **Consideration**: May need stimulus change

### Declining Trend
- Readiness scores decreasing
- **Action**: Investigate causes (fatigue? technique regression?)
- **Warning**: May indicate overtraining or need for recovery

## Integration with Training Load

Coach AI uses readiness to recommend:
- **Session type**: Recovery, Technique, Movement, Full, Match-sim
- **Intensity**: Low, Moderate, High
- **Focus areas**: What to work on
- **Avoid areas**: What to rest

This guidance is personalized to your current state.

## Realistic Expectations

Few athletes are at "Excellent" constantly:
- Fluctuation is normal
- Training creates temporary fatigue (readiness may dip)
- Recovery improves readiness
- Progressive overload: stress → recover → adapt

Target: Rising average over weeks/months, not perfection every day.

## When Readiness Seems Wrong

### Feels Better Than Score
- Score may be detecting subtle issues you don't feel yet
- Trust the data but also your body
- Consider both objective and subjective assessment

### Feels Worse Than Score
- Psychological factors not captured (stress, sleep, motivation)
- Illness or injury not visible in biomechanics
- Always prioritize how you feel

Readiness is one tool. Combine it with self-awareness and professional guidance.

## Key Takeaway

Match readiness is a training guidance signal, not a verdict. Use it to:
- Optimize training timing
- Plan competitions strategically
- Detect overtraining early
- Build confidence through preparation

Combined with your experience and coaching, it helps you make better decisions.
